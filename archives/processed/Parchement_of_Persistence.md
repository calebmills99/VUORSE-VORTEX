## Scene: The First Steward Transmission at Eisensang

The weight of Hildebrand's ring pressed against his collarbone where it hung on its chain, warm from his skin despite the drafts creeping through the stone corridors of Schloss Eisensang. He traced the engraving—an interlocking spiral of thread and mirror—with his thumb out of habit, then lifted his head sharply when a gust shuddered the iron candleholder beside him. Its light wavered across the cavern's polished black floor, throwing his reflection into flickering fragments.

Margarethe's voice drifted from the arched doorway behind him, dry as parchment.

"The Vessel's echo only answers when you stop demanding it."

She stepped forward, her mourning veil whispering against the shoulders of her embroidered coat. The scent of crushed myrtle rose from her sleeves—always myrtle, never rosemary. Her fingers hovered near his, not touching the ring. Not yet.

Hildebrand turned slightly.

"You and the Weaver. Always watching."

Margarethe's breath caught—just once, sharp enough that her veil trembled against her lips. Her fingers, still hovering near his, twitched as though she were resisting the urge to snatch the ring away. Candlelight fractured across her face, catching the silver-threaded embroidery of her sleeves, where tiny mirrors sewn between the stitches threw slivers of Hildebrand's reflection back at him.

"Not the Weaver," she said at last, voice lower now, pressed flat against the cavern's cold air. "That name isn't hers to claim yet."

Her thumb brushed the spiral engraving without touching his skin, tracing the path of the thread like she was following a map she had hoped never to need again. The candle guttered, and for a heartbeat, the shadows made her pupils look like twin keyholes.

"What brings you down here to our cavern, old pal?"

Hildebrand code-switched easily, slipping into the relaxed speech of their own era, the one to which they were both now refugees.

Margarethe's shoulders loosened by half an inch—the closest she ever came to laughing in these caverns. She exhaled through her nose, stirring the veil just enough to reveal the ghost of a smile at the corner of her mouth. Candlelight caught the fine wrinkles around her eyes as she tilted her head, the silver threads in her sleeves shimmering like a distant river seen from horseback.

"Our cavern," she repeated, her voice warmer now, edged with the old Hooplehopper lilt that used to curl through feast-hall gossip.

Her fingers finally closed around the ring—not seizing, but cradling, her thumb pressing the spiral engraving into his palm like a secret returned. The draft shifted again, sending shadows shuddering across the cavern walls in slow, liquid ripples.

"I know you've just been itching to break out into the Charleston or a Lindy Hop."

Margarethe's fingers tightened reflexively around the ring—not enough to hurt, but enough that the metal bit briefly into his palm. Her veil shifted as she shook her head, the black silk catching the candlelight like oil on water.

"You always did have the timing of a drunkard at a funeral," she murmured.

The tension in her shoulders melted into something looser, almost fond. The embroidered mirrors on her sleeves winked as she shifted her weight, her slippers scuffing against the cavern floor in a half-aborted step that might, in another world, have been the beginning of a twirl. The draft carried the scent of myrtle again, sharper now, as if she had crushed the leaves between her fingers when he spoke.

"Ah. It would seem I have once again overlooked a missive of some sort?"

Hildebrand did not enjoy having to guess Margarethe's intent. She had become even more obscure since the Crystal Lattice Incident. Almost ephemeral.

Margarethe's veil lifted slightly as she exhaled through her nose—that particular Hooplehopper sigh reserved for when someone underestimated her archival precision. The fingers not currently wrapped around Hildebrand's ring dipped into her embroidered sleeve and emerged with a square of parchment so thin the candlelight bled through its watermarked edges.

"Third shelf," she said, tapping the corner against his wrist, where three pinprick holes formed a precise triangle. "Left of the rosewood astrolabe."

The scent of iron gall ink rose from the page as she turned it, revealing the faint but unmistakable latticework diagram pressed into the fibers—not drawn, but embedded, as if the paper itself had grown around the pattern. Her thumb brushed one intersecting line where the grid warped slightly, like fabric stretched over a hidden seam.

Hildebrand opened the parchment and stared in disbelief at a perfect dot-matrix printed model of a Slayton lattice structure.

"But how did you—"

The parchment trembled slightly in his grip as the candlelight revealed the latticework's impossible precision—each dot aligned with geometric cruelty, forming a structure that should not exist outside Federstahl's shattered experiments. Margarethe's sleeve brushed his wrist as she withdrew her hand, leaving behind the scent of myrtle and something sharper, like the ozone crackle of a storm pressing against castle walls.

Her reflection fragmented in the cavern's polished floor, warped by the uneven surface until she appeared less a woman and more a series of black-veined angles.

"Paper remembers," she said, too softly for the words to echo.

The candle guttered suddenly, plunging the lattice diagram into stark relief. For one heartbeat, the dots glowed like embers, searing afterimages across his vision. Then it was just parchment again, save for three pinpricks where her fingers had pressed, damp with what might have been ink or blood.

"You've discovered another artifact? Erm, relic?"

Hildebrand knew better than to ask. Margarethe could not stand stupid questions. He braced for impact.

Margarethe's fingers twitched toward her sleeve—not the reflexive grab of anger, but the controlled withdrawal of someone shelving a knife. Her veil shifted as she tilted her head, studying him with the same detached precision she once used to inventory the Hooplehopper archives. The parchment between his fingers crisped slightly at the edges, as if reacting to the sudden dryness in the cavern air.

"Neither," she said, voice cool as the myrtle oil she used to polish mirrors.

One embroidered sleeve drifted toward the lattice diagram—not touching it, but hovering close enough that the tiny mirrors stitched into the fabric threw fractured light across the dots.

"Just an old family recipe. You'd know that if you ever read past the first line."

The last word landed with the weight of a ledger slammed shut, but her reflection in the cavern floor showed her thumb worrying at a loose thread in her cuff—a tell she had not had since childhood.

"Well, then. Let's keep this between us for now. We barely escaped that last round of burnings. Those poor servants. I warned them to hold their tongues."

Margarethe's veil went utterly still—that unnatural frozen drape that meant she was counting breaths the way other people counted coin. The parchment between his fingers crisped further at the edges, the lattice dots darkening as if absorbing the cavern's damp. Her reflection in the polished floor fractured into smaller and smaller shards, until all that remained was a mosaic of black silk and the occasional glint of a stitched mirror.

"Too late for warnings," she said at last, voice pared down to the barest inflection.

The candle flame dipped as she stepped closer, her embroidered hem whispering against stone. Her fingers—cold, always so cold since the Lattice Incident—brushed the parchment's edge without taking it back.

"They held their tongues. Just not tight enough."

The last word ghosted across his knuckles, light as the ash still clinging to her sleeves from that distant pyre.

Hildebrand felt his skin erupt into a cold chill, goosebumps forming as the ring grew hot around his neck and began to sear his flesh. He ripped it from around his neck and threw it across the cavern into the darkness, where it continued to glow.

The ring clattered against the cavern wall with a sound like a cracked bell, skittering across polished stone before coming to rest in a shallow pool of groundwater. Steam rose where the glowing metal met liquid—hissing faintly as the water bubbled against whatever infernal heat now lived inside Hildebrand's ancestral sigil.

Margarethe's veil whipped toward the sound, her embroidered sleeves lifting as if to shield her face from the sudden radiance pulsing from the discarded ring. The lattice diagram in his hand crisped further at the edges, its precise dots darkening to match the rhythm of the ring's throbbing light—a syncopated heartbeat that made the cavern's mirrored surfaces ripple like disturbed mercury.

Margarethe's reflection fractured into a dozen jagged pieces as she stepped back, her blackened fingernails catching the erratic light. The ozone scent intensified, threading through the myrtle oil clinging to her veils until the air tasted like a storm about to break.

When she spoke, her voice came from everywhere and nowhere, the cavern's acoustics bending each word into overlapping echoes.

"You never could bear its weight when it remembered."

The ring's glow pulsed once, violently, before dimming to an ember's smolder—illuminating just enough to show the way its heat had warped the stone beneath it into glassy, rippled waves.

As she spoke, they both heard something they should not have been hearing. Could not have been hearing. The mere implications of it were enough to drive any person mad.

It was the indistinguishable sound of a dot-matrix printer.

Busy.

Printing.

"Open the parchment now."

The sound clicked through the cavern like a mechanical spider spinning its web, each percussive strike of the printer head syncing with the ring's fading pulses in the pool. Margarethe's veil stiffened mid-sway as the noise resolved into an unmistakable rhythm: the staccato percussion of an impact printer from three centuries unborn. The parchment in Hildebrand's hand vibrated subtly, its lattice dots rearranging themselves into fresh configurations with every mechanical thwack from the darkness.

Her reflection in the glassy floor shattered completely as she pivoted toward the sound, sleeves whispering against stone in perfect counterpoint to the printer's relentless cadence. The air thickened with ozone and something sharper—the scent of overheating circuitry where no circuits should exist.

When the voice spoke again—"Open the parchment now"—it emerged from the cavern walls themselves, each syllable syncopated with the printer's hammering like a punch card feeding through steel rollers. The ring's glow flared once more, illuminating the way its heat had fused stray droplets of groundwater into tiny lenses across the stone, each one reflecting a different fragmented angle of Margarethe's frozen silhouette.

Hildebrand, as always, had to be witty even in moments of abject terror.

"I think that message is for you, old gal."

Margarethe's veil snapped toward him with the whip-crack precision of a falconer's glove, her reflection in the glassy floor reassembling into a single, razor-edged silhouette as the printer's mechanical stutter faltered mid-cycle. The ozone scent spiked as her embroidered cuffs flared open, revealing wrists lined with tiny mirror shards that caught and refracted the ring's dying glow into disjointed Morse code across the cavern walls.

"Hil-de-brand," she enunciated, each syllable colder than the groundwater steaming around the discarded ring.

The printer resumed with a violent clatter, its rhythm now synced to the twitch of her blackened fingernails against her sleeves, as if the machine in the dark were an extension of her fury. The parchment in his hand went preternaturally still, its lattice dots freezing into a configuration that unmistakably spelled SEAM in bastardized Carolingian minuscule.

Hildebrand opened the parchment begrudgingly, knowing that whatever happened next would mark both of them in ways yet unknown. As he laid it flat, Margarethe anchored it with a heavy candelabra.

As the parchment caught the light, words that had been there all along resolved from the grain. Not written. Not arriving. Waiting.

Hello, children.

The candelabra's weight stretched the parchment taut just as the dots reconfigured, ink swimming like mercury to form letters too crisp for quill or press. Margarethe's breath caught audibly when the salutation resolved, her veil trembling inches above the page as if repelled by its very geometry.

Hello, children glowed faintly now, each serif edged in the same unnatural radiance as the ring still cooling in its pool.

Her reflection fractured again in the polished floor—but this time, the shards rearranged into a mosaic of eyes, all staring at the parchment with pupils dilated black as the cavern's depths. The printer's clatter accelerated to match her pulse, its rhythm now synced to the twitch of her embroidered hem where it brushed his wrist. Steam rose from the candelabra's base, its wax melting too fast, pooling around the anchoring point in viscous spirals that mirrored the lattice pattern now pulsing beneath the words.

The letters continued to appear.

Welcome to the Order of the Seam.

As your servants, poor Isolde and Yorick, can attest, we have reached an inflection point. My name is Caleb Mills Stewart. As the Weaver's Steward Vessel, it is my duty to inform you that the Order of the Seam must be established posthaste to prevent any further disaster. Not just for your own sakes, but for the entire Hooplehopper diaspora.

Yes, there are more of you.

It's practically a party!

Margarethe's veil stiffened completely as the words unfurled, her reflection in the glassy floor splintering into a dozen jagged silhouettes that each mimicked her recoil. The candelabra's wax pooled faster now, rivulets carving paths through centuries of dust to form branching patterns that mirrored the lattice beneath Caleb Mills Stewart's declaration.

Her embroidered cuffs fluttered, not from any cavern draft but from the sheer force of her exhale—a sharp scent of crushed myrtle and iron blooming as the printer's staccato rhythm stuttered mid-sentence. The ring's glow pulsed weakly in its pool, casting wavering light across the parchment where Hooplehopper diaspora lingered like a brand.

Her blackened fingernails twitched toward the page but did not touch, hovering just above the ink that still shimmered with the same uncanny radiance as the cavern's mirrors. The printer fell silent at last, leaving only the sound of wax dripping onto stone in perfect sync with the ring's fading heartbeat.

"Well, here we are then. The founding members of the Order of the Seam. I guess that means I have to learn how to sew now."

Hildebrand quipped.

Margarethe's veil tilted slightly—the first genuine movement since the printer fell silent—as the candlelight caught the minute tremor along its hemline. The wax pooling around the candelabra hardened abruptly into jagged fractals, mirroring the sudden tension in her shoulders. Her reflection in the cavern floor distorted, elongating like molten glass before snapping back into a single, sharp-edged silhouette.

A breath passed.

Two.

Then the corner of her mouth twitched upward. It was not quite a smile, but something loosened in the air between them as the last of the parchment's unnatural glow faded to ordinary ink. The cavern's acoustics amplified the quiet tap of her fingernail against the candelabra's base, a dry counterpoint to the distant plink of groundwater hitting the ring's cooling metal.

"So what do we do now? Isolde would have known precisely what to do. But without her, we've lost our emissary to this era."

Hildebrand winced at the echoed sound of her screams that had pierced the night air just a fortnight hence.

Margarethe's veil dipped toward the parchment as the printer's last tremor faded, her reflection in the cavern floor flickering with the candlelight's uneven pulse. The iron scent intensified when she exhaled through her nose, fingers curling around the candelabra's base hard enough to leave wax crescents in the blackened metal.

A draft snaked through the cavern, making the candle flames gutter. For three breaths, the only sound was water dripping onto the ring—each plink timed perfectly with the echo of Isolde's last scream still vibrating in Hildebrand's ribs. Margarethe's silhouette blurred momentarily at the edges, as if the cavern itself hesitated to define her shape without Isolde's laughter to anchor it.

"Ah. Silence. Which, with you, Margarethe, is an answer in and of itself. I'll take on the weight of this."

As he spoke, he dipped into the darkness and returned with the ring back around his neck, in its rightful place.

"You could sense something was about to happen with the ring. That's why you were pawing at it. You could have warned me."

The ring settled against his collarbone with a weight that had nothing to do with gold, its warmth leaching through fabric as Margarethe's veil rippled in a nonexistent draft. Her reflection fractured across three separate mirrors, each shard angled to show a different version of her fingers curling—not in denial, but in the precise motion she had used to unpick the Order's first cipher from Isolde's sleeve the night before the screaming.

Wax pooled around the candelabra's base in slow spirals, its scent suddenly sharp with crushed fennel where earlier it had been myrtle.

Her silence stretched as the cavern's acoustics amplified every shift: the creak of Hildebrand's gambeson as he straightened, the wet plink of a single droplet hitting the ring's surface, the way her embroidered hem trembled against the stone floor like a needle hesitant to pierce silk.

When she finally moved, it was to trace one blackened nail along the parchment's edge—leaving a streak that glowed faintly where it crossed the word diaspora before fading into ordinary ink.
